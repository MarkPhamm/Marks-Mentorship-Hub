I put my mentorship on one page: markmentorship.com

Since 2020 I've been mentoring students and early-career people into data roles — data analyst, analytics engineer, data engineer. The guides were always public on GitHub. Now there's a front door.

The whole thing runs on one rule: if you do what everyone else does, you will not get hired.

Recruiters scroll past hundreds of profiles that look exactly the same. "Aspiring Data Analyst | SQL · Python · Power BI", a Titanic notebook, a template portfolio. Not wrong — just invisible.

So every session comes back to two questions:
- The copy-paste test: could 1,000 other candidates paste this line unchanged?
- The only-me test: what is true about you that isn't true about them?

How it works: you read the playbook, you do the reps (our Slack bot posts everyone's commits daily — the group sees your streak), and I review the hard parts.

No application form. Add me with a note. The note is the application — and your first copy-paste test.

markmentorship.com
